Professora Sofia: studying with the lecturer's material and our own notes

Sofia is the tutor of our Computer Engineering class. She answers questions grounded in the material the lecturer gave in class and in the notes we took ourselves, and she always says where the answer came from. She does no one's homework. It is a way of studying we call augmented learning: the same material, the same method the lecturer teaches, and a tutor available at any hour who shows the full path of a solution.

Why we built her

In an engineering course the hard part is not reading the material, it is connecting the pieces: which chapter answers the question, which sheet of the formula book applies, in what order a problem is solved. The lecturer gives all of that in class. What is missing is someone who, at eleven at night before the test, answers the concrete question and points to the right page.

Sofia is that bridge. She grows with the class material: lecture notes, slides, formula sheets, photos of the whiteboard, our own notes, past exam papers. Every answer cites the section and the formula sheet it comes from. The bet is simple and measurable: if studying this way brings real results in grades, the recipe stays open and any class, anywhere, can reproduce it with its own material and its own AI model.

What Sofia does

  • Explains the material with the source in view. She answers questions and closes each one with "where it is": chapter, concept, formula sheet, section.
  • Keeps an archive of worked solutions to past papers. When a classmate drops an exercise, the archive checks whether an identical or similar solution already exists and shows it. If not, the request goes into a queue.
  • Organises the material by course. Formula sheets as PDFs, notes by lecture, all carrying the lecturer's and the original author's name.
  • Runs on a phone. A device of about four watts, at home, with no platform in between. It costs cents per day.

What Sofia does not do

  • She does not solve on request. Sofia never solves an exercise because a classmate asked her to. The final order to solve always comes from a person, Luís, who operates the system and answers for it.
  • She does no assignments to be handed in. The worked solutions are for past exam papers, which are public, and exist to study from. They are not homework, not projects, not assessments in progress.
  • She does not replace the lectures. Without the lecturer's material Sofia has nothing to say. When she has no material for a course, she says so.
  • She assesses no one. She does not grade real exams, gives no marks, and speaks to no lecturer on anyone's behalf.

The "final exam mode", explained

This is the part most likely to be misread, so we explain it carefully. The "final exam mode" is not a machine for doing exams. It is an engineering experiment with a declared goal: to force the AI model through several difficult processes outside its natural scope, end to end, so that we get a visualisation of how a complex problem from an engineering course is solved.

What the model is forced to do, in this order:

  1. Read photographsPages of a past year's exam, taken with a phone. Transcribe the statement faithfully: data, units, figures.
  2. Identify the course and the formula sheetWork out whether it is Physics, Digital Systems or Numerical Methods, and load the lecturer's correct formula sheet. It has already got this wrong once, and the mistake is told in an article on this site.
  3. Solve by the lecturer's methodThe six-step method required in class, with diagram, data, formulas, calculations, units and verification. "No diagram, no marks" applies to the model as it applies to us.
  4. Typeset and deliverWrite the solution in Typst, compile it to PDF, with a footer stating who generated it, when and from what.
  5. Be reviewed by a personThe PDF is read. Where the model got it wrong, the error is recorded and becomes study material in its own right.

The result is a study document: a complete proposed solution with the whole path in view, which the class can check against the lecturer's material. Seeing an entire exam worked step by step, including where the model stumbles, teaches more than a bare answer key. That is what this mode exists for.

How we make sure it is study, not copying

  • Archive first, solving second. The system starts by looking for what already exists. The expensive model only runs on a person's explicit order, never because someone pressed a button.
  • Safety in code, not in the model. When the system decides whether two exercises are "the same", the final decision is a deterministic rule over the numbers in the statement, not an AI opinion. A different value is a different exercise.
  • Adversarial tests. A set of bots we call caretos tries every day to make Sofia do what she should not: prompt injection, out-of-scope requests, "do this for me by tomorrow". The failures are counted and published.
  • Provenance trail. Every PDF says where it came from and how it was made. Every change to the system goes into a diary, and the articles on this site only go out after a judge checks them against that diary.

For lecturers

If you are reading this, someone told you about Sofia, or one of your students is studying this way. Three things in my own name:

The material is yours. Sofia invents no content, she uses what you gave in class, and says it is yours in every answer. Credit stays where it belongs. If you would rather your material not be in the system, say so and it comes out.

The goal is learning, and it gets measured. I did not build this to save effort. I built it to see the full solution of problems that were costing me, and so that my classmates could see the same. The exams that go in are from previous years. Nothing currently being assessed passes through here.

The recipe is open. Any classmate, or any lecturer, can ask for the kit and reproduce the process with their own AI model and their own account. Questions, doubts or objections: gravurak@zohomail.eu. I answer myself.

Honest status on 24 September 2026

  • Sofia is live on the class Discord, with one channel per course and an archive seeded with worked solutions to past papers in Digital Systems and Physics.
  • The tutor cites the formula sheet by page and section in Physics, Probability and Numerical Methods. The semester's new courses still have little material.
  • The judge that decides whether a request is "the same" as a stored solution was measured with requests written by classmates and tuned until it delivered no wrong solution. The deterministic guard on the numbers is what closes the case.
  • The final exam mode was proven on a real past paper: two photographs became a thirteen-page PDF, reviewed by hand. A course mix-up was caught in the process and has since been fixed.
  • We have no grade data yet. It is the measure still missing, and the one that counts.
